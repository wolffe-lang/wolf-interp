### 3.2 Where shard 1's time goes: `run_corpus` walks the corpus a dozen times

Each of `run_corpus`'s walking tests evaluates every corpus entry itself:
`entries()` (7 tests), `unsafe_free_corpus_programs_never_produce_a_ub_verdict`
(the safe-tier entries), `every_unsupported_file_says_why`,
`every_record_is_schema_valid_including_the_running_ones` (1 each), and
`evaluation_is_deterministic_over_the_corpus` (2). That is about twelve
evaluations of the whole corpus, on four threads: three waves of four
walks. At 725d86da (run 42621872032): wave one ended at 5,520–5,539 s,
wave two at 11,093–11,169 s, and wave three was cancelled at 15,873 s,
about 780 s short of its end. `divergence` (one walk on one thread, plus a
lex-and-parse walk) took 3,777 s in the same job, so a wave of four
concurrent walks costs 1.46 times one walk alone.

Per test, per OS, per corpus: `summary.py`'s section 3. On windows, the
determinism test (two walks) is twice the others, every era.

`ci/is85/is85_timing.rs` (a measurement harness, never a test target)

it, in the debug test profile CI builds. On one macOS arm64 workstation
(`local-walk-<head>.tsv`):

| head (files) | one walk | the heaviest files |
|---|---|---|
| 6240efe5 (1225) | 908 s | `conc/par_fail_reraises` 234, `conc/par_order` 229, `memory/byte_list_ledger` 188, `memory/byte_producers_ledger` 99, `conc/atomic_race_plain` 80: 829 s, 91 % |
| ed711840 (1239) | 1,333 s | the same five, unchanged to within 2 %, and the new `memory/region_outer_write_push_copy_call` 418 s |
| 17bd5223 (1253) | 1,373 s | the same six; `region_outer_write_push_copy_call` 464 |
| 1ed91d14 (1253) | 1,332 s | the same six; `region_outer_write_push_copy_call` 420 |

(725d86da's walk is a Linux host's, at opt-level 0 beside the opt-level
rows of §4.3; its unoptimized walk is not compared across hosts.)

Each heavy program is a loop of tens of thousands of container method
calls (`(mut xs).push(…)` 65,536 or 100,000 times). In a debug build every
such call also keeps a copy of its receiver (`src/eval/mod.rs`, the
`#[cfg(debug_assertions)]` arm that checks the lend's O(1) mutation
witness against the copy), so the loop is quadratic there and linear in a
release build: at the current corpus the release `place trace identity`
step evaluates the whole corpus twice, as processes, in 9–17 minutes on
windows, where the debug `corpus harness` step's two in-process walks
(`lupin corpus` observes every entry twice, once for its listing and once
for its tally) take 71–133.

### 3.4 Is the growth the corpus, lupin, or the runner?

- **The step on 11-01 is the corpus: one file.** Every OS's corpus-walking
  binaries grew 1.3–1.6 times between the last nightly at a5378e3f
  (6240efe5) and the first at b16353e8 (ed711840): macOS, the steadiest
  runner, `run_corpus` 5,905–6,653 s → 9,608–10,855 s, `divergence`
  937–1,116 → 1,420–1,711. The local walk says why: the 1,225 files both
  heads share took 908.0 s and 913.0 s (+0.5 %), and the fourteen new
  files took 419.5 s, 417.8 of it `memory/region_outer_write_push_copy_call.lu`
  (a 100,000-byte value pushed byte by byte, then copied by a collector).
  One walk grew 908 → 1,333 s, 1.47 times. The release place-trace step,
  which has no quadratic receiver copy, did not move (16 min before and
  after).
- **lupin did not get slower per file**, across that step or after it:
  the walks at 17bd5223 (11-06) and 1ed91d14 (11-07), on the 1,253-file
  corpus, took 1,373 and 1,332 s against ed711840's 1,333 for 1,239, every
  heavy file within 3 % but one, whose 464 s at 17bd5223 was 420 at the
  next head (noise; one take each). macOS's hosted `run_corpus` agrees
  (next point). No commit range to name, so no issue for a lupin slowdown.
- **11-07 to 11-09 is the runner.** The contract's three nightlies share
  one corpus (5f1f1c94, 1,253 files). At that corpus macOS's `run_corpus`
  stays within 9,287–10,188 s (1.10×), while windows's same binaries spread
  1.6–2.0× from night to night (`divergence` 1,930 → 3,777; `doc_truth`
  6,172 → 10,429; `run_corpus` 9,326 → past 15,873). The windows fleet has
  fast and slow machines, and the slow ones are about 1.7–1.9 times the
  fast. The fix has to fit the slow ones.
- **The file count is not the lever.** The 223 files of 10-25 (1002 →
  1225) moved the windows shards' medians by under 5 % (the ladder's, which
  also runs every file as a process, by 17 %); the 14 of 11-01 moved every
  job by a third or more.

Every heavy entry, not shard 1 alone, is past 220 on a slow machine, so a
lever that moves only `run_corpus` cannot pass the gate.

- **(B) `run_corpus` walks once.** Its twelve walks become one shared walk
  and the determinism test's second, each on the runner's four cores, the
  two at once. `ci/is85/walk-model.py` replays the measured per-file walk
  (§3.3) on four cores with the measured contention (1.46): today's shape
  costs 4.38 times one walk alone (the 11-07 and 11-08 nightlies measured
  4.83 and 4.39 times their `divergence`), and the shared shape 0.88 at the
  current corpus (0.73 at a5378e3f, before the 418 s file arrived), about
  five times less. **On the slowest machine seen (one walk 3,777 s),
  `run_corpus` about 3,300 s, from about 16,700; shard 1 about 121
  minutes.** Shards 2 and 3 and the ladder do not move: still 302–330, 307
  and 292. Every OS gains: `run_corpus` is the largest binary on all three.
- **(A) optimized code, debug assertions and overflow checks kept.** The
  heavy entries' `cargo test` and `cargo run` build with
  `opt-level = N` and nothing else changed: `debug-assertions` and
  `overflow-checks` stay on (the receiver copy of §3.3 and every
  `debug_assert!` still run; `wolf_interp::BUILD_PROFILE` still reads
  `debug`). Every corpus-walking binary and ladder step divides by the
  factor the walk gains (2.45 at level 2, measured in §4.3), at the cost
  of a longer build.
- **(C) `lupin corpus` observes each entry once.** It observes every entry
  twice (§3.3). Once would halve the ladder's `corpus harness` step (about
  −65 minutes on the slowest machine) and take one walk out of
  `doc_truth` and `cli` each. This is `src/main.rs`, the product, not the
  test schedule.
- **(D) more windows jobs.** A fourth shard and the ladder in two (+2
  windows jobs a night and a release, and new entries for the plan, the
  coverage proof and the bundle comparison). Without (A), the heavy
  binaries' slowest total after (B) and (C) is about 620 minutes, and
  `export` alone is 157 of it, so even four shards sit near 160–180 each,
  and the ladder's halves near 80 (the corpus harness and the fast rungs)
  and 155 (the place trace and the bundle).
